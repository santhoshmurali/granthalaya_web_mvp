> "I bow to the Guru Raja of Granthalaya, who bestows great auspiciousness, possesses extraordinary glory, abounds in noble qualities, and, through the grace of Sri Hari, grants us well-being in this world and the world beyond." — Sri Granthalaya Guru Stotram

## A guiding mantra

*"Our Granthalaya shall be like Mantralayam; within Granthalaya there shall be a Mantralayam."*

Sri Raghavendra Granthalaya is a temple founded upon sacred books. It makes visible, before our eyes, what those books describe. Its founder, Amman Sathiyanathan, wrote that to research and write sacred books is one undertaking and to study them is another, but to unite and build a temple that gives living form to what those books describe is something altogether different.

## The tradition behind it

An Acharya must not merely teach; he must live what he teaches. Sri Madhvacharya corrected misreadings of the scriptures, and Sri Jayatirtha, Sri Vijayindra and many other yatis presented Dvaita Siddhanta in an accessible way. In that succession the saint of Mantralayam, Sri Raghavendra, authored fifty works that made those principles easy to understand and rooted in our hearts. These masters are the *Vyasa Kuta*; the Haridasas who sang the same philosophy in devotional compositions are the *Dasa Kuta*.

## A call from childhood

The darshana of saints and Haridasas drew the founder to devotion from his earliest years, inspired by Sri Satyanatha Tirtha of Veeracholapuram. He progressed from stories to poetry and drama. Then a crushing blow came: his younger brother was gravely injured in an accident, and betrayal and harsh accusations left him in despair.

In 1977 he travelled to Mantralayam for the first time, praying that his brother be saved. Guru Raja saved his brother, granted him darshana, revealed his story and set him on the path that led, step by step, to this Granthalaya.

## How the Granthalaya began

From 1978 the founder wrote and published books on Mangadu Amman. Later Sri Guru Raya appeared in a dream to his sister Vijaya and asked that he write about Him too. That became the multi-volume research work *Sri Raghavendra Mahimai*.

Readers who understood the labour behind it began to offer donations on their own. The founder distributed books free to those who could not afford them and kept the remaining funds in a fixed deposit. Toward the end of 2007, at Cameron Highlands in Malaysia, he heard a voice repeating, *"Granthalaya, Granthalaya, Granthalaya."*

In November 2008 the Sri Raghavendra Granthalaya charitable trust was established. The inaugural function was held on 31 December 2008 at the Sri Matha in Ayanavaram.

## The land

In March 2009 a well-wisher offered land. The founder visited Tiruvallur, below Poondi Lake, and felt a quiet fulfilment. The donors offered an acre; the gift deed was executed on Akshaya Tritiya, 27 April 2009, and the property was registered in the trust's name on 18 March 2010. It was, just as he had inwardly wished, the land behind the shrine of the village deity Pasur Amman. Everything was His grace.

## Built by devotees

Devotees from Chennai to Singapore, Dubai, Malaysia, France, the United Kingdom, Canada, the United States and Australia have given their seva. Among the gifts: the majestic replica of the Mantralayam Brindavana and the seven-and-a-half-foot Sri Avatara Traya Anjaneya; the shrines of Sri Dvaraka Krishna and Sri Rukmini; the lifelike Sri Nijarupa Raghavendra; the rajagopuram, the Great Chariot, a Sivalinga worshipped by the Kanchi Paramacharya, and the form of Sri Shankukarna. Even today, Guru Raja continues to accept countless services through countless loving devotees.

## Kumbhabhishekam and the road ahead

- **9 February 2017**: first Kumbhabhishekam, conducted by the head of Udupi Palimaru Matha.
- **22 February 2024**: second Kumbhabhishekam under Sri Vidyadhisha Tirtha and the junior pontiff Sri Vidya Rajeshwara Tirtha.
- **Third phase**: an annadana hall, a large kitchen, expansion of the great assembly mandapa and a shrine for King Bahlika.
- **Fourth phase**: the Parimala Mandapa, a library on the second floor, an exhibition of artist Sasi's paintings and a lift toward the gopuram.
- **By 2029**: twelve years after the first Kumbhabhishekam, a Maha Kumbhabhishekam.

## Why "Parimala"

Sri Rayaru bears the title *Parimalacharya*. The sacred offering at Mantralayam is *Parimala Prasada*, and those who serve Rayaru at Granthalaya receive the honour *Parimala Sri*. *Parimala* also means a sweet and pleasing fragrance, and the commemorative souvenir *Granthalaya Parimala Maala* was made to spread that fragrance across time.
